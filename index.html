## The user's question (2026-11-19 12:28 JST, translated)

The user is worried about the quality of videos we deliver to paying clients. His proposed workflow:

1. Read the client's brief and conditions first.
2. Produce the video.
3. Instead of handing the finished video to another AI directly (which he expects to hit lane limits), use the bridge design we already use for text packets: we create a link, place the deliverable there, and have the AI come and fetch it.
4. Receive the AI's evaluation, revise, and only then submit to the client.

His two questions: (a) can this workflow actually be run? (b) is the judgment itself correct — he has asked us to put that to the other AIs and proceed on their answers.

This round is a DESIGN consultation only. No real client file is uploaded anywhere in this round.

## Current state (measured facts, not estimates)

- **Internal verification already exists and is measurement-based.** Production and verification are always separate agents. The verifier re-measures the FINISHED file against QC items ①–⑯: stream/duration integrity, full Whisper transcription vs script, loudness (LUFS-I ≥ −17), frame inspection (head/middle/chapter heads/end), subtitle safe-zone (bottom edge ≥25% from screen bottom) and wording identity with the narration, subtitle speed caps, physical plausibility of imagery, platform spec conformity, compliance wording (PR labels, no effect promises in fortune content), and conformity with the genre's design spec. A pass is required ≥3 hours before a scheduled publication. This system was built for our OWN channels' videos; it has never yet been applied to a paid client delivery.
- **Client-work track record is shallow.** First contract ever: a test script delivery (CrowdWorks, ¥110 tax incl.; inspection completed 2026-11-18, ¥86 paid after fees). Second contract: a single thumbnail image as a paid test (CrowdWorks case 13608052; contract + escrow completed 2026-11-19 10:50 JST; delivery due within 5 days of escrow, i.e. by ~2026-11-24). There is NO delivered client video yet. The first client videos are expected to be short-form edits (Shorts/Reels class) under the bounded track-record lane (one-off, ≤2 hours of work or ≤1 Short, work-in-progress ≤3).
- **The user's worry is therefore about a near-future, low-volume flow**: a handful of client videos per week at most, each small, each against a written client brief (length, format, captions, references, deadline).
- **Lane limits, measured:**
  - Claude Free (disposable lane): a 5-hour message window; ~10 messages per window observed (limit hit 2026-11-19 03:46 JST, reset 08:20 JST).
  - Gemini API: 20 generateContent requests/day, SHARED across all work (reviews, benchmarks, everything); the budget is ledger-managed and the provider count governs.
  - ChatGPT (disposable lane): fetching a TEXT packet from a public link is PROVEN (2026-11-16 known-answer test, 3/3 correct via normal browsing of a public link). Fetching and actually watching a VIDEO from a link is UNVERIFIED.
  - Gemini API connector additionally offers video understanding for public YouTube URLs (connector capability, used before for public videos); it does not read arbitrary mp4 links.
- **Confidentiality facts.** Client deliverables can include client-supplied materials (raw footage, logos, product images, unreleased campaign information). Client work is contracted inside CrowdWorks; the deliverable is the client's property on payment. Our bridge host (GitHub Pages) is PUBLIC: anyone with the URL can fetch the page, and URLs can leak, be indexed, or be re-shared. There is no access control, no expiry enforcement, and no takedown speed guarantee.
- **Zero spend** applies to any design proposed: no paid storage, no paid API, no paid tier of any lane.

## Binding resources (resource decomposition)

Any design must be priced against these denominators, not per video alone:

1. **Lane quota units.** One external review consumes: 1 Gemini API request (of 20/day shared) and/or 1 Claude message (of ~10 per 5h window, shared with all other consultation) and/or one ChatGPT lane session (operator time on the PC lane). Revision loops multiply this.
2. **Coordinator + PC operator time** per round-trip (publishing the artefact, driving the lane, extracting and verifying the reply, dispositioning points).
3. **Delivery lead time.** Client deadlines are hard (the current test: 5 days from escrow). A review step that can stall on a lane limit (NOT OBTAINED) interacts with the deadline.
4. **The user's hours: zero by design.** Nothing in the design may require the user to upload, relay, or check anything.

## Framing pre-mortem (assumptions this packet's frame smuggles in)

- **A1.** That an external AI review adds detection power beyond the internal QC for the defect classes that matter in client work (brief conformity, subjective "does this look professional", client-taste fit). The internal QC is measurement-based and strong on measurable defects; the marginal value of an external pass is UNPROVEN, and this packet's framing presumes it.
- **A2.** That "the lane fetched the link" equals "the lane watched the video". Text fetch is proven; video watching is not, and a fluent review can be generated from the brief + transcript + a few frames without the video ever being seen. The frame treats retrieval as observation.
- **A3.** That an obscure public link is an acceptable container for a client's not-yet-delivered property because it is unlikely to be found. Obscurity is not access control; the frame under-weights the confidentiality and contract side.

## Questions

**Q1 — Is the judgment itself correct?** Given that a measurement-based independent internal QC already exists, SHOULD an external AI evaluation be inserted before client submission? What defect classes would an external lane plausibly catch that the internal verifier structurally misses (e.g. brief-conformity judgment, subjective quality, first-impression impact)? What will the external lane structurally miss or get wrong (e.g. anything requiring actual frame-accurate measurement, loudness, subtitle timing)? Answer for client short-form video work specifically, not for our own channels.

**Q2 — Per-lane feasibility of "come and fetch it".** For each lane (ChatGPT disposable web lane, Claude Free web lane, Gemini API): can it actually fetch and WATCH a video placed at a link we create? Compare three placements: (a) a direct public mp4 link; (b) a review page that places side-by-side the video + the client's brief + the script + the transcript + representative frames; (c) an unlisted YouTube link. Which placements yield a review grounded in the actual video, and which yield a plausible review hallucinated from the surrounding text? How can we VERIFY the AI actually watched (e.g. requiring timestamped observations only obtainable from the video, seeded details, questions whose answers are not in the transcript)? State per lane what is proven, what is plausible, and what is UNCONFIRMED.

**Q3 — Confidentiality line.** The deliverable is the client's property and may embed client-supplied materials. What may NEVER be placed on a public link? **Q4 — Process design.** If an external review step is adopted: before or after the internal verifier's pass? What revision-loop cap (how many external rounds per delivery before we ship on internal QC alone)? Answer against hard client deadlines and the account-safety / no-waiver stance in the premises brief: note that the internal QC gate is non-waivable, while the external step would be new — should it be a gate at all, or advisory?

**Q5 — Cost against the quotas.** Price the design per delivered video, in the binding units above: API requests, Claude window messages, ChatGPT lane sessions, coordinator minutes. At the expected near-term volume (a few client videos per week, possibly rising), does a per-delivery external review crowd out the lanes' other duties (mistake rounds, milestone audits, routine first reviews)? What frequency is sustainable — every delivery, first-delivery-per-client only, sampling, or on-demand when the internal verifier flags uncertainty?

**Q6 — Frame challenge (mandatory).** What is the coordinator (Muse) missing? Which perspectives has the coordinator failed to take in framing this question — about the value of external review, about what clients actually judge, about the confidentiality exposure, or about anything else? What variable is missing, what assumption is smuggled in, and what would make this whole question the wrong question?

**Q7 — Better alternatives.** If you judge the proposed design wrong or weak, propose the strongest alternative that achieves the user's actual goal (confidence that a client video is good before submission) inside the premises: zero spend, disposable lanes only, no client material on public links if Q3 so finds, internal QC gate untouched.
